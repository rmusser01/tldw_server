The attribution failure has a concrete pipeline boundary. [The section-summary implementation](../../tldw_Server_API/app/core/Research_Workspace/section_summaries.py) divides a selected source into fixed 24,000-character portions and sends each slice independently to extraction. It retains an author only when that literal name occurs inside the slice. Synthesis receives generated summary/claim text and nonempty attribution fields, but no explicit statement that the ordered portions are processing divisions rather than contributor divisions. A missing byline in a continuation can consequently become a false claim about another author. This is distinct from the valid-ID check, which also cannot establish that a cited quote supports its claim.

A bounded follow-up would identify ordered processing portions with their shared source section and ranges, and explicitly prohibit inventing contributor boundaries or denying an established attribution merely because a continuation lacks a repeated byline. It must not blindly inherit the first author: EXIM, Trade and Financial Regulatory Agencies have genuine contributor changes. No such prompt or attribution repair has been made or validated in this run. Offline contract checks could verify the supplied context; semantic improvement would still require a targeted saved-evidence retest and human review.
